How your events and mood went together

Each bar shows how your mood tended to compare with your typical on days with one more event. Bars inside the grey zone mean “about the same.”

Did you know? Two people can go through the exact same thing and feel totally different about it. A fight with a friend might ruin one person’s whole day, while someone else shakes it off by lunch. Neither is right or wrong; that’s just how different people are. Scientists are even curious whether these differences might go along with differences in health, which is one of the things Rise to Health is studying. These bars show how your mood tended to move with your days.

What this can and can’t tell you. These bars show what happened on the same days, not what caused what. For example, if you were in a better mood on days you helped others, helping may have lifted your mood, or you may have been more likely to help when you were already in a good mood.

A note on these numbers

This report describes about two weeks of your own days, so your patterns might look different over another two weeks. We did not run statistical tests on one person’s data, so these patterns are descriptions of what happened, not proof that one thing affects another. When we looked at how things went together, we also softened any very unusual day or night a little, so that a single one doesn’t decide the whole pattern.

When we combine data from all the teens in Rise to Health, we can test questions like these for teens in general, and then look at how they relate to everyone’s blood work to see which ones matter for heart health. Those are the findings we’ll share in our scientific publications. Stay tuned!
